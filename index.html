Five ways in. Two to test first.

Ranked by fit with your experience and by capital and construction risk — not by proven profit. Traditional flipping is a comparison, not the default. Flip a card, read the test, and pick any path you want to keep in play. Your picks stay on this device.

Investor property research

Prepare investor-ready reports on public listings: comparable sales, risks, and inspection findings. Low starting capital and little construction exposure.

Path 01 · The test

Will someone pay?

Would investors pay for a research package rather than doing the analysis themselves? Stay with research and administrative support until any brokerage-like work is legally reviewed. One later idea: use a live listing, after your input, to test whether an actual investor would pay — without buying a house.

Path 02Start here

Commercial property activation

Help commercial owners get spaces ready: vendors, occupancy requirements, and tenant onboarding. A possible fit with Rosa’s facilities and operations work.

Picked
Path 02 · The test

A paid problem

Can you find owners or organizations with a documented problem they would pay to solve? Programs such as Motor City Match show a local ecosystem, not proof that a private paid service would sell. Any private work must respect boundaries around Rosa’s nonprofit role.

Path 03

Deal sourcing / wholesaling

Find discounted opportunities, negotiate legitimate contracts, and possibly assign interests where the law allows. Lower capital, higher legal and transaction risk.

Picked
Path 03 · The test

Legal first

A genuine buyer, a sound deal, and an approved legal structure. Michigan rules on brokerage, advertising, and assignment need a lawyer’s review first. Land Bank contracts may not allow a quick wholesale.

Path 04

Light residential renovations

Buy homes that need limited cosmetic work, use qualified contractors, and resell or rent. A familiar model — and it brings back contractor oversight and cost overruns.

Picked
Path 04 · The test

Does the math survive?

Can a property stay profitable after realistic bids, holding costs, financing, selling expenses, and a contingency? This path takes more capital and more execution risk — the work you asked not to take on yourselves.

Path 05

Land Bank rehabilitation

Buy eligible distressed properties through the Detroit Land Bank and complete required improvements. Opening prices can look very low; as-is condition, title, rehab, and deadlines usually dominate the real cost.

Picked
Path 05 · The test

All-in, before a bid

A realistic all-in cost and completion schedule before any bid. We would not bid on the houses in the map below yet. Program rules can include progress reporting and a six-month rehabilitation and occupancy target; missing them can put ownership at risk.

05 — 30-day plan

A month to test, not to buy.

A short validation sequence. The aim is one repeatable, legally workable opportunity with a willing customer and acceptable economics — not a cheap house in week one.

Week 1

Discovery

Confirm time, capital, income goals, preferred work, and geography. Review 10–15 property or service prospects. Do not purchase property in this phase.

Week 2

Demand

Talk with at least five prospective customers — investors, commercial owners, or operators. Write down the problems they already pay to solve and what they would pay.

Week 3

Feasibility

Underwrite the strongest opportunities with sold comparables, real cost estimates, legal review, and pricing. Choose a model based on evidence.

Week 4

A small pilot

Seek a paid research assignment, a commercial activation engagement, or another clear demand signal. Do not buy a property just to show activity.
